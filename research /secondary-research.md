# SellerPilot: Secondary Research

## Research Overview

**Research type:** Secondary research (publicly available evidence)

**Research focus:** Missing-delivery disputes in ecommerce.

**Platforms under investigation:** Etsy, Shopify, and eBay. The primary platform has not yet been selected.

**Perspectives:** Online buyers and small ecommerce sellers.

## Research Question

How do buyers and sellers experience and resolve situations where an online order does not arrive, and what opportunities exist to improve transparency, communication, and decision-making?

## Research Methods

- Review publicly available buyer and seller discussions.
- Examine official platform policies and dispute-resolution workflows.
- Compare existing ecommerce support tools.
- Identify recurring themes, workflow gaps, and design opportunities.

## Research Limitations

This study uses secondary evidence rather than direct participant interviews. Public complaints represent individual experiences and cannot establish how frequently a problem occurs. Findings will be distinguished from assumptions and proposed solutions.

### E01: Etsy Missing-Delivery Resolution Process

**Research date:** October 11, 2026
**Source organization:** Etsy
**Source type:** Official platform help documentation (primary source for Etsy's stated procedures)
**Publication/last-updated dates:** Not clearly displayed on the pages reviewed
**Date accessed:** October 11, 2026

#### Sources

1. Etsy. *How to Get Help with An Order*. https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402660818583-How-to-Get-Help-with-An-Order
2. Etsy. *How to Open a Case*. https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/5745586898199-How-to-Open-a-Case
3. Etsy. *How to Resolve a Case from a Buyer*. https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016126873-How-to-Resolve-a-Case-from-a-Buyer
4. Etsy. *How to Answer a Help Request from a Buyer*. https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/13241489600919-How-to-Answer-a-Help-Request-from-a-Buyer

#### Verified observations

- [1] Etsy directs buyers experiencing an order problem to contact the seller through the order's help process.
- [2] For physical orders, the estimated delivery date must have passed and 48 hours must have elapsed after a help request before a buyer can open an eligible case. Other eligibility conditions may apply.
- [2] Etsy identifies non-delivery as a case category and states that qualifying orders may receive a refund under Purchase Protection.
- [3] Etsy states that order shipping status and Purchase Protection eligibility are used to determine many case outcomes. Sellers may be asked for additional evidence, including proof of shipment or tracking.
- [3, 4] Sellers can communicate about cases and may need to respond to requests for additional information.

#### UX interpretation (not validated)

The documented workflow involves several stages: seller contact, eligibility waiting periods, possible escalation, evidence review, and resolution. These stages create questions worth investigating about clarity of next steps, status visibility, and the responsibilities of buyers and sellers.

#### Research gaps

- Do buyers understand when they are eligible to escalate a case?
- Do sellers know what evidence they need to provide?
- Which steps, if any, cause delays or confusion?
- How do buyers and sellers perceive the fairness of the outcome?

#### Potential design opportunity (hypothesis only)

Explore whether a shared, guided case overview could help users understand case status, required evidence, responsibilities, and next steps. Do not treat this as a validated need.

#### Limitations

These official pages describe Etsy's stated processes, not how successfully those processes work in practice. They do not establish user satisfaction, problem frequency, or actual usability difficulties. Current documentation should not be assumed to describe Etsy's policies in 2018.


### E02: Buyer Uncertainty During a Delayed Etsy Delivery

**Source author:** Anonymous Reddit user (original poster)
**Source title:** As a buyer, what would you do?
**Platform:** Reddit, r/Etsy
**Publication date:** October 1, 2026
**Date accessed:** October 11, 2026
**Source type:** Public buyer-reported experience (anecdotal)
**Original URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/Etsy/comments/1wvep0p/as_a_buyer_what_would_you_do/

#### Reported observations

- The buyer reported receiving a shipping notification from Etsy while carrier tracking still showed a label-created or pre-transit status.
- The buyer described contacting the seller and receiving assurances about shipment or replacement.
- The buyer expressed uncertainty about whether to open a case and concern about losing the opportunity for resolution.

#### UX interpretation (not validated)

The reported mismatch between platform notifications and carrier tracking may contribute to uncertainty about an order's actual status. The buyer's concern about case timing suggests that escalation guidance and deadlines are worth investigating.

#### Research gaps

- Was the package actually handed to the carrier?
- What information was visible in Etsy's order and help interfaces?
- Was the buyer eligible to open a case at that point?
- How was the issue ultimately resolved?

#### Potential design opportunity (hypothesis only)

Explore whether clearer distinctions between label creation, carrier acceptance, and delivery, alongside case eligibility guidance, could help buyers decide what to do next.

#### Limitations

This is one self-reported public discussion, not a verified interview or representative study. The seller's perspective and the final resolution are not independently established. No conclusions about prevalence or platform-wide usability can be drawn from this case.

### E03: Seller Experience with Missing-Delivery Disputes
**Source author:** Reddit user (original poster; username available at source)
**Source title:** Missing Large Order
**Platform:** Reddit, r/EtsySellers
**Publication date:** April 27, 2026
**Date accessed:** October 12, 2026
**Source type:** Public seller-reported experience (anecdotal)
**Original URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/EtsySellers/comments/1sxk4p7/missing_large_order/

#### Reported observations

- The seller reported shipping a $450 order of seven items with tracking.
- According to the seller, the carrier marked the order as delivered, but the buyer said it had not arrived.
- The seller described contacting the buyer about checking with neighbours, household members, and the carrier.
- The seller expressed concern about the financial consequences of a refund and the effect of negative reviews.

#### UX interpretation (not validated)

A seller may face conflicting information between carrier tracking and a buyer's account. Resolving such cases can involve evidence gathering, communication, financial decisions, and concerns about customer trust.

#### Research gaps

- Was the order delivered to the correct location?
- What evidence could the seller or buyer access?
- Was the order eligible for Etsy Purchase Protection?
- What was the final resolution?

#### Potential design opportunity (hypothesis only)

Explore whether an evidence-focused dispute overview could help sellers understand available information, financial implications, and possible next steps before deciding how to respond.

#### Limitations

This is a single self-reported account. The buyer's perspective, delivery evidence, and final outcome are not independently verified. It does not establish the prevalence of this problem among Etsy sellers.

### E04: eBay Missing-Delivery Resolution Process
**Source organization:** eBay
**Source type:** Official platform help documentation
**Date accessed:** October 14, 2026
**Publication/last-updated dates:** Not clearly displayed on the pages reviewed

#### Sources

1. eBay. *Get help with an item that hasn't arrived*. https://www.ebay.com/help/buying/returns-refunds/item-not-received?id=4042
2. eBay. *Help a buyer with an item they didn't receive*. https://www.ebay.com/help/returns-refunds/returns-refunds-sellers/item-not-received?id=4116
3. eBay. *Tracking items you've sold*. https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/managing-orders/resolving-issues-with-buyers?id=4088

#### Verified observations

- [1] Buyers can report a missing item after its estimated delivery date and generally must report within 30 calendar days to remain eligible for buyer protection.
- [1] Buyers can request an update, replacement, or refund from the seller.
- [1, 2] Sellers generally have three business days to respond before eBay can be asked to intervene.
- [2] Sellers can provide tracking details, issue a refund, or communicate with the buyer to resolve the request.
- [2, 3] Tracking evidence is important to seller protection. eBay specifies carrier and delivery-information requirements; some high-value orders require signature confirmation.
- [1] eBay provides a process for buyers to check their request status and escalate unresolved cases.

#### UX interpretation (not validated)

The process requires buyers and sellers to understand deadlines, tracking evidence, and escalation options. A potential design question is whether this information is sufficiently clear at the moment users need to make decisions.

#### Comparison with E01 (Etsy)

Both platforms document seller-first resolution followed by potential platform escalation. Their eligibility rules, evidence requirements, and protection programs differ. This comparison establishes procedural similarities, not shared usability problems.

#### Research gaps

- How clearly are deadlines communicated within the live interface?
- How do sellers interpret tracking evidence when buyers dispute delivery?
- Which steps do users actually find difficult, if any?

#### Limitations

Official documentation describes stated platform procedures, not actual user experiences or the frequency of disputes. Requirements may vary by case and can change over time.

### E05: Shopify Missing-Delivery Resolution Process

**Source organization:** Shopify
**Source type:** Official platform documentation
**Date accessed:** October 14, 2026
**Publication/last-updated dates:** Not clearly displayed on the reviewed pages

#### Sources

1. Shopify. *Contacting a Shopify store about an order*. https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/contacting-shopify-store
2. Shopify. *Order tracking at Shopify*. https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/order-status-page/order-tracking
3. Shopify. *Refunding orders*. https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/orders/refund-cancel-order
4. Shopify. *Submitting shipping claims*. https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/fulfilling-orders/shipping-labels/shipping-carriers/shipping-claims

#### Verified observations

- [1] Customers purchasing from Shopify-powered stores are directed to contact the individual merchant for help with their orders. Merchants establish their own store policies and business practices.
- [1] Shopify provides an issue-reporting form for customers who have contacted the merchant and received no response or resolution for at least 30 days. This should not be confused with a universal refund guarantee.
- [2] Customers can access shipment tracking through the store's order-status page, shipping emails, and the Shop app, depending on the available tracking information.
- [3] Merchants can issue full or partial refunds from Shopify Admin.
- [4] Merchants may be able to submit shipping claims for missing or damaged shipments, depending on the carrier, label, and insurance coverage. Claim procedures vary.

#### UX interpretation (not validated)

Because independent merchants manage customer relationships and refunds, a missing-delivery issue may require coordination between the merchant, customer, shipping carrier, and insurance provider. The documentation suggests several possible information sources and decision points, but does not establish that merchants find these difficult to manage.

#### Comparison with E01 (Etsy) and E04 (eBay)

Etsy and eBay document platform-managed buyer-seller case processes with eligibility and escalation rules. Shopify provides tools for independent merchants to manage orders, tracking, refunds, and certain shipping claims. These are differences in service structure, not evidence that one platform provides a better user experience.

#### Research gaps

- How do small merchants currently coordinate missing-delivery investigations?
- Which tasks, if any, require switching between tools?
- How do merchants decide when to refund, replace, or pursue a shipping claim?
- What existing Shopify apps already support these workflows?

#### Potential design opportunity (hypothesis only)

Investigate whether small merchants would benefit from clearer coordination of tracking evidence, customer communication, refund options, and shipping-claim information. Do not treat this as a validated need.

#### Limitations

These sources document Shopify's available capabilities and stated procedures. They do not establish how frequently merchants experience missing-delivery problems, how usable the existing tools are, or whether an additional product is needed. Capabilities and claim eligibility can vary by merchant and shipping arrangement.
